# Research Protocol: Language Processing in Cognitive Psychology

## Purpose

This protocol supports empirical and simulated research on language processing as the coordinated cognitive system that supports comprehension, production, lexical access, parsing, meaning construction, pragmatic inference, discourse integration, reading, speech processing, and communication. It is intended for cognitive psychologists, psycholinguists, language scientists, literacy researchers, cognitive neuroscientists, AI researchers, speech-language researchers, and HCI researchers.

## Core constructs

### Lexical access

Lexical access refers to the retrieval of stored word forms, meanings, grammatical properties, and associated knowledge from long-term memory.

### Word frequency

Word frequency measures how often a word occurs in a language environment. Higher-frequency words are typically recognized more rapidly and accurately.

### Lexical ambiguity

Lexical ambiguity measures the degree to which a word or phrase has multiple possible meanings, increasing interpretation demand.

### Syntactic complexity

Syntactic complexity measures the structural difficulty of a sentence or phrase, including embedding, dependency length, ambiguity, and grammatical relations.

### Semantic predictability

Semantic predictability measures how strongly a context constrains or anticipates a word, meaning, or interpretation.

### Context support

Context support measures the degree to which prior discourse or situational context helps resolve ambiguity and construct meaning.

### Working-memory load

Working-memory load measures the burden of maintaining partial linguistic structures during comprehension or production.

### Pragmatic inference demand

Pragmatic inference demand measures the extent to which interpretation requires inferring speaker intention, implied meaning, social context, or discourse purpose.

### Discourse coherence

Discourse coherence measures how well an item fits with prior text, narrative structure, conversational flow, or topic continuity.

### Production latency

Production latency measures the time required to move from communicative intention or prompt to a spoken or written response.

## Recommended study designs

1. Lexical-decision experiment with word-frequency and semantic-priming manipulation.
2. Self-paced reading or eye-tracking study with syntactic-complexity manipulation.
3. Sentence comprehension study with working-memory load and ambiguity.
4. Pragmatic inference study comparing literal and implied meaning.
5. Discourse coherence experiment across context-support conditions.
6. Language production study measuring naming latency, error, and fluency.
7. Human-AI comparison of comprehension, ambiguity resolution, and generated-language evaluation.

## Measurement cautions

- Do not treat reading speed alone as comprehension.
- Separate lexical access from sentence-level integration.
- Separate syntactic complexity from semantic predictability where possible.
- Model participant and item effects because words and sentences vary in frequency, length, ambiguity, familiarity, and plausibility.
- Track accuracy alongside response time.
- Distinguish literal comprehension from pragmatic interpretation.
- Consider linguistic diversity, dialect, multilingualism, literacy experience, and accessibility.

## Ethical and institutional considerations

Language processing research intersects with education, literacy, disability, translation, speech-language pathology, interface design, AI systems, and public communication. Researchers should avoid treating one language variety, dialect, accent, literacy background, or institutional language norm as the default measure of cognitive capacity.
