# Measurement Notes

## Analytic judgment

J* = f(x1, x2, ..., xn)

where all relevant cues are weighted or evaluated.

## Heuristic judgment

J_h = g(x1, x2, ..., xk), k < n

where the heuristic uses fewer cues or a simpler transformation.

## Effort-accuracy utility

U = A - lambda E

where A is expected accuracy, E is cognitive effort, and lambda controls the cost of effort.

## Availability

p_hat(event) ∝ R(event)

where judged probability depends on retrieval ease.

## Anchoring and adjustment

x_hat = a + delta

where a is the anchor and delta is adjustment away from the anchor.

## Anchoring bias

B_anchor = x_hat - x_true

or, when comparing high and low anchor conditions:

A_effect = mean(x_hat_high_anchor) - mean(x_hat_low_anchor)

## Representativeness-base-rate integration

p_hat(category | case) = alpha * similarity(case, category) + (1 - alpha) * base_rate(category)

Higher alpha indicates stronger reliance on representativeness.

## Recognition heuristic

If one of two alternatives is recognized and recognition validity is high, infer that the recognized alternative has the higher criterion value.

## Take-the-best

Search cues in order of cue validity and stop at the first cue that discriminates.

## Tallying

Choose the option with the largest count of favorable cues, ignoring cue weights.

## Fast-and-frugal tree

Sequentially inspect a small number of cues; exit early when a cue triggers a decision.

## Calibration error

calibration_error = |judged_probability - true_probability|

## Adaptive fit

adaptive_fit = accuracy_heuristic - accuracy_benchmark

Positive values indicate that the heuristic outperforms the benchmark in the observed environment.

## Recommended reliability checks

- Split-half reliability for judgment tasks
- Test-retest reliability for stable heuristic tendencies
- Participant-level random effects
- Item-level random effects
- Outlier checks for anchors and response times
- Manipulation checks for recall ease, fluency, affect, and time pressure

## Recommended validity checks

- Criterion validity against true probabilities or expert ratings
- Process validity using response time, cue search, or verbal protocol
- Ecological validity using real-world cue structures
- Discriminant validity across heuristic types
- Sensitivity analysis comparing heuristic and weighted-additive models
