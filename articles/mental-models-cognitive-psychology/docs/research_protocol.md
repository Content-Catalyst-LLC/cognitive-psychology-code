# Research Protocol: Mental Models in Cognitive Psychology

## Purpose

This protocol supports empirical and simulated research on mental models as internal representations that allow people to explain, simulate, predict, and intervene in systems. It is intended for studies of reasoning, system understanding, learning, decision making, HCI, human factors, AI-assisted work, and complex-domain education.

## Core constructs

### Model completeness

Model completeness measures whether a participant represents the relevant elements, variables, states, actors, or components in a system.

### Model coherence

Model coherence measures whether the represented relations among components form a consistent and usable structure.

### Causal-link accuracy

Causal-link accuracy measures whether the participant identifies correct causal relations among variables.

### Feedback-loop recognition

Feedback-loop recognition measures whether the participant detects reinforcing or balancing feedback processes.

### Boundary accuracy

Boundary accuracy measures whether the participant includes relevant system boundaries and excludes irrelevant features.

### Prediction error

Prediction error measures the gap between a participant's predicted state and the observed or correct state.

### System-understanding score

System-understanding score summarizes model completeness, coherence, causal accuracy, feedback-loop recognition, and explanatory quality.

### Model revision

Model revision measures whether a participant updates the mental model after feedback or prediction failure.

### Transfer

Transfer measures whether a model built in one context supports reasoning in a structurally related context.

### Intervention choice accuracy

Intervention choice accuracy measures whether the participant selects an action that follows from an accurate model of system behavior.

## Recommended study designs

1. System-prediction task comparing novice and expert mental models.
2. Causal-diagram elicitation study before and after instruction.
3. HCI study comparing user mental models against designer conceptual models.
4. Complex-system learning study measuring feedback-loop recognition.
5. Model-revision experiment after outcome feedback.
6. Transfer study across structurally similar but superficially different systems.
7. Human-AI decision-support study measuring calibrated model use and overreliance.
8. Policy, infrastructure, ecological, or organizational scenario study comparing causal assumptions with observed outcomes.

## Measurement cautions

- Do not treat confidence as model accuracy.
- Separate model completeness from model coherence.
- Track prediction error, not only self-reported understanding.
- Measure revision after feedback.
- Use item and participant effects because system tasks vary strongly in difficulty.
- Distinguish a learner's mental model from an expert model, a designer model, and a formal computational model.
- Consider language, culture, professional background, institutional role, and domain familiarity.

## Ethical and institutional considerations

Mental models are not neutral when they shape policy, diagnosis, interface design, organizational strategy, or public communication. Flawed models can misdirect resources, obscure marginalized experience, and preserve institutional error. Researchers should examine who gets to define the system, whose causal knowledge is included, and which feedback loops remain invisible.
