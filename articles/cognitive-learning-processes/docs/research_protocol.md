# Research Protocol: Cognitive Learning Processes

## Purpose

This protocol supports empirical and simulated research on cognitive learning: how attention, working memory, encoding, prior knowledge, schemas, retrieval, feedback, cognitive load, and transfer shape durable understanding.

## Core constructs

### Prior knowledge

Prior knowledge describes the learner's existing conceptual, procedural, and contextual knowledge before instruction or practice. Prior knowledge can support new learning, but it can also mislead when schemas are inaccurate.

### Attention score

Attention score measures whether relevant information was selected, sustained, and available for encoding.

### Encoding quality

Encoding quality measures how effectively information is transformed into meaningful representations.

### Schema strength

Schema strength measures the organization and usability of a learner's knowledge structure.

### Retrieval practice

Retrieval practice records whether learning involved active recall rather than exposure alone.

### Cognitive load

Cognitive load measures processing burden imposed by task complexity and instructional design.

### Transfer score

Transfer score measures whether knowledge learned in one context can be applied to novel problems or settings.

### Retention score

Retention score measures delayed recall, recognition, reconstruction, or application after time has passed.

### Learning gain

Learning gain measures improvement from baseline to post-learning performance.

## Recommended study designs

1. Prior-knowledge and new-learning study.
2. Retrieval-practice versus rereading experiment.
3. Worked-example and problem-solving comparison.
4. Cognitive-load and instructional-design experiment.
5. Spacing and interleaving study.
6. Transfer study comparing surface similarity and deep-structure recognition.
7. Retention study after delay.
8. Human-AI learning-support study evaluating whether tools support durable knowledge.

## Measurement cautions

- Do not equate exposure with learning.
- Do not treat immediate fluency as durable retention.
- Distinguish recognition, recall, explanation, transfer, and adaptive application.
- Model participant and item/task effects.
- Track response time and cognitive load alongside accuracy.
- Consider unequal prior knowledge, language background, access, disability, educational opportunity, and feedback quality.

## Ethical and institutional considerations

Learning outcomes are shaped by environment, instruction, feedback, resources, time, language access, and psychological safety. Researchers should avoid framing learning differences solely as individual deficits when instructional design and institutional conditions may be major causal factors.
