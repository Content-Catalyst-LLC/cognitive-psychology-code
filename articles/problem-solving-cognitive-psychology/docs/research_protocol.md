# Research Protocol: Problem Solving in Cognitive Psychology

## Purpose

This protocol supports empirical and simulated research on problem solving as a flexible, goal-directed cognitive process. It is intended for cognitive psychologists, learning scientists, education researchers, decision scientists, AI researchers, human factors specialists, and organizational researchers.

## Core constructs

### Problem representation

Problem representation measures how accurately and usefully the solver encodes the initial state, goal state, constraints, operators, and relevant relations.

### Goal clarity

Goal clarity measures how clearly the desired outcome is specified or understood by the solver.

### Constraint load

Constraint load measures the number, complexity, and rigidity of constraints that limit the available solution path.

### Working-memory load

Working-memory load measures the amount of active information the solver must maintain while searching, comparing, and evaluating candidate moves.

### Strategy type

Strategy type records whether the solver uses algorithmic search, heuristic search, means-end analysis, analogical transfer, insight, trial-and-error, decomposition, or mixed strategies.

### Strategy switching

Strategy switching measures whether and when the solver abandons an ineffective strategy and adopts a new one.

### Metacognitive monitoring

Metacognitive monitoring measures the solver's awareness of progress, impasse, confidence, error, and strategy effectiveness.

### Solution accuracy

Solution accuracy indicates whether the response reaches the correct or intended goal state.

### Solution quality

Solution quality measures adequacy, efficiency, elegance, generality, robustness, or expert-rated quality of the solution.

## Recommended study designs

1. Controlled comparison of strategy types across difficulty levels.
2. Working-memory load manipulation during problem solving.
3. Problem-representation intervention study.
4. Insight versus analytic problem comparison.
5. Dynamic problem-solving simulation with uncertainty and feedback.
6. Expert-novice comparison across domain-specific problems.
7. Human-AI decision support experiment measuring strategy selection and reliance.

## Measurement cautions

- Do not equate speed with problem-solving quality.
- Separate representation quality from final accuracy.
- Separate strategy selection from strategy execution.
- Model participant and item effects because problem difficulty varies strongly.
- Preserve failed attempts, switches, impasse points, and hints.
- Distinguish open-ended, ill-structured, and well-structured problems.

## Ethical and institutional considerations

Problem solving is often treated as an individual skill, but real problem-solving performance depends on tools, institutions, education, time, resources, psychological safety, and unequal access to support. Researchers should avoid interpreting poor performance as individual deficit when the problem environment is poorly structured or unfairly burdensome.
