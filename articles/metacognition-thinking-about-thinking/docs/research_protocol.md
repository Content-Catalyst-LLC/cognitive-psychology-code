# Research Protocol: Metacognition

## Purpose

This protocol supports empirical and simulated research on metacognition as the monitoring and control of cognition. It is intended for studies of learning, problem solving, decision making, expertise, reasoning, uncertainty, confidence, and self-regulated cognition.

## Core constructs

### Confidence

Confidence measures a participant's subjective belief that a response, memory, judgment, or strategy is correct.

### Accuracy

Accuracy measures objective performance on a task. Accuracy and confidence should be analyzed separately because people can be confident and wrong, uncertain and correct, or well calibrated.

### Calibration error

Calibration error measures the absolute gap between subjective confidence and objective performance.

### Overconfidence and underconfidence

Overconfidence occurs when judged performance exceeds actual performance. Underconfidence occurs when judged performance falls below actual performance.

### Monitoring sensitivity

Monitoring sensitivity measures whether confidence varies appropriately with correctness, difficulty, feedback, and evidence quality.

### Control and regulation

Control refers to strategic changes triggered by monitoring, such as reviewing, slowing down, switching strategies, seeking help, reallocating study time, or deferring judgment.

### Judgments of learning

Judgments of learning estimate future recall or understanding after study.

### Feeling of knowing

Feeling of knowing judgments estimate whether currently unrecalled information would be recognized later.

### Strategy shift

Strategy shift indicates whether a participant changed their approach after detecting uncertainty, error, low confidence, or low progress.

## Recommended study designs

1. Confidence-accuracy calibration task.
2. Judgment-of-learning and delayed recall study.
3. Feeling-of-knowing and recognition study.
4. Strategy-shift experiment with feedback.
5. Self-regulated learning study using restudy choice or study-time allocation.
6. Problem-solving task with impasse, progress monitoring, and strategy revision.
7. Decision-making study measuring uncertainty and evidence-seeking.
8. Human-AI decision-support study measuring calibrated reliance and confidence.

## Measurement cautions

- Do not treat confidence as accuracy.
- Preserve both signed calibration and absolute calibration.
- Model participant and item effects.
- Track response time, difficulty, feedback, and strategy changes.
- Separate monitoring from control where possible.
- Avoid treating poor metacognition as an individual moral deficit; task design, feedback quality, time pressure, stress, education, and institutional context shape monitoring and control.

## Ethical and institutional considerations

Metacognition is not only an individual skill. It is supported or undermined by learning environments, feedback systems, psychological safety, accessibility, assessment design, workload, and institutional incentives. Researchers should examine whether environments make accurate self-monitoring possible.
